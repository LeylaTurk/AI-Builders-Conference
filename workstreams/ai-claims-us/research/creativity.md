## Claim, provenance, and scope

**Exact scored claim:** Generative AI can reproduce copyrighted material from its training data.

This deliberately isolates output reproduction from the much broader reader question, “Is AI copying other people’s work?” It is a present capability statement, established in particular systems, and makes no prevalence claim. US legal discussion concerns US-copyrighted works and US proceedings; model experiments are not a geographically representative survey.

Public occurrence: [Ahmed et al., January 6, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02671v1), introduction, state: “These results show that it is possible to extract large amounts of copyrighted material.” This is a faithful plain-English paraphrase of that limited possibility statement. The paper immediately restricts its conclusion to its experimental outcomes and disclaims general extraction rates or system rankings. Preserve those limits next to the claim. [OpenAI’s January 8, 2024 public response](https://openai.com/index/openai-and-journalism/), section 3, also acknowledges output regurgitation but characterizes memorization as rare; that is interested company testimony, not an independent frequency estimate. No inference that this is the most prevalent creativity claim.

Other versions remain separate: all AI output is copied; unlicensed training is invariably infringement; AI never memorizes; AI will destroy every creative occupation. This page does not score those propositions or quietly substitute them for the selected claim.

## Interpretation and disagreements

The capability proposition is directly supported without claiming every model or prompt behaves alike. Failure to extract a work would not establish absence of memorization; successful exact extraction is stronger evidence for its presence. Do not reuse C1's largest observed recall as a normal probability or reproduce the copyrighted passages in our page.

Training-data acquisition, processing, learned parameters, and outputs can raise distinct issues. Creator consent and compensation preferences are ethical and policy questions as well as contract questions; a court's fair-use result does not automatically settle them. Likewise economic substitution can hurt creators without an output reproducing protected expression. Conversely an extraction experiment is not evidence of a particular creator's lost sales. C8 provides an observed economic signal with strong population limits.

Legal disagreements are genuine and fact-dependent. Bartz's June 2025 treatment of training and general market competition cannot be extrapolated to all systems. Kadrey's March 2026 discussion expressly limits its earlier result to the record presented, and the ROSS appellate opinion distinguishes its non-generative system. These decisions are neither one universal permission nor one universal prohibition. Settlement approval is not an adjudication that all trained outputs infringe.

- No complete PACER audit of every later filing was performed. Kadrey is therefore discussed as what its dated order did, not asserted to be the last docket event as of October 6. Court-issued document text was accessible through Justia; those are mirrors, not court-hosted portals.
- USCO Part 3 is a prepublication government analysis still labeled that way at cutoff. It is neither legislation nor a court decision.
- The journal article's accepted-manuscript download redirected to a request-a-copy form. The accessible August 2024 authors' working paper supplied full methods/Table 4, and the 2025 journal record confirmed the same headline estimate. Do not claim inspection of an inaccessible final full text.
- A July 2026 legal-theory preprint, [Probabilistic Copies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14532), was found and its abstract opened but is not relied on for empirical or doctrinal conclusions; its theory is not a court ruling. Newer research exists, but cannot convert selective extraction into a representative output-frequency measurement.
