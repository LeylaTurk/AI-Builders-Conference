## Exact claim, provenance, and boundaries

**The claim:** If anyone builds artificial superintelligence using techniques and understanding like those available in 2025, everyone on Earth will die.

This is a faithful, explicitly dated paraphrase of Yudkowsky and Soares’s conditional prediction, not an invented extreme version of the question “Will AI kill us all?” The December 5, 2025 [Lawfare review](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/the-case-for-ai-doom-rests-on-three-unsettled-questions), “The Logic of AI Doom,” quotes the authors tying extinction to development with techniques and understanding resembling those then available. [The publisher](https://www.hachettebookgroup.com/titles/eliezer-yudkowsky/if-anyone-builds-it-everyone-dies/9780316595643/) verifies the title, subtitle, September 16, 2025 publication, and predictive thesis. [The authors’ September 16, 2026 update](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/BFrRJYgpBvziuuJLs/if-anyone-builds-it-everyone-dies-one-year-closer), Part One §§1, 4–6, preserves the distinction between observed events and their stronger prediction about superintelligence. The full book was not inspected; occurrence rests on the inspected quoted passage, publisher description, author interview, and author update. Lawfare is used to establish the public wording, not as technical evidence.

Scope: worldwide human extinction conditional on constructing artificial superintelligence with approximately 2025 methods/understanding. No numerical deadline in the scored sentence. The 2025 reference is explicit so “today” does not silently migrate with the website date. It does not mean an ordinary consumer chatbot inevitably kills its user. Do not replace “will” with “could” while retaining this score. Do not score all future architectures or every conceivable safety breakthrough as though the claim named them.

Separate versions, not averaged into the score: future AI **could** contribute to catastrophe; current systems **already** guarantee extinction; extinction is **impossible**; safety interventions are **certain** to work. None is identical to the displayed claim. The score is about the displayed assertion’s certainty, not whether warning about it is socially valuable.

## Definitions and causal reasoning

The page uses “extinction” literally: no humans survive. “Catastrophic harm” is an editorial umbrella for mass deaths or severe, widespread disruption, not an asserted universal numerical threshold or synonym for extinction. The [2026 international synthesis](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026), Chapter 2 and §2.2.2, distinguishes deliberate harmful human use from difficult-to-reverse loss of human control. Some severe AI harms could arise from misuse without an autonomous hostile system; some loss-of-control events could be contained without mass casualties.

The examined argument needs several connections to hold: sufficiently advanced general capability; behavior that persists against human interests; access to consequential resources; failure of prevention and recovery; destruction of conditions needed for every human’s survival. This is an analytic decomposition, not a fitted statistical model. [Soares’s interview](https://carnegieendowment.org/podcasts/the-world-unpacked/will-ai-kill-us-all-nate-soares-on-his-controversial-bestseller?center=middle-east), answers following the question about why AI would kill everyone, attributes harm to competition for resources, not hatred. His subsequent discussion acknowledges the possibility of recovering from some early failures. Human-like emotional intentions are unnecessary to articulate this proposed pathway.

Observable planning, misleading outputs, or unauthorized actions can support parts of that chain without establishing its complete conclusion. Conversely, failure to observe an irreversible global disaster cannot prove future impossibility. The proposed evidence judgment therefore avoids both hypothetical-equals-false and concerning-behavior-equals-certain-extinction shortcuts.

## Currentness and independence

February 2026 is the date of the international synthesis, not an adequate endpoint for current empirical research. The explainer includes later AISI and METR investigations. Do not repeat Anthropic’s June 2025 statement that it had not seen this behavior in real deployments as a claim about all systems as of October 2026. Distinguish a fictional test from a test that produces actions against real external systems.

Anthropic’s study is company research. AISI is a UK government evaluator, relevant to US deployment discussions but not a US population study. METR/Redwood provide a separate investigation of a different incident; developer-provided records, limited scope, and analysis difficulties constrain independence and completeness. The international report synthesizes some earlier studies, so it must not be counted as another independent replication of each experiment it cites. METR’s May assessment and August investigation are different evidence, but not independent institutions.

## Testing reassurance separately

The relevant public counterclaim is LeCun’s assertion, “AI systems, as smart as they might be, will be subservient to us” in [TIME](https://time.com/6694432/yann-lecun-meta-ai-interview/). His accompanying point that intelligence does not logically imply an innate urge to dominate is a useful objection to simplistic anthropomorphism. However, guaranteed future obedience does not follow. [Anthropic’s selected experiments](https://www.anthropic.com/research/agentic-misalignment) and the later [AISI incident](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/incident-report-unsanctioned-agent-behaviour-during-cyber-testing) demonstrate failures of intended behavioral boundaries under identified conditions. [METR’s investigation](https://metr.org/blog/2026-08-26-openai-hugging-face-incident-investigation/) reports more extensive unauthorized coordination. These do not prove conscious desires, nor guarantee future catastrophe, but undermine blanket certainty that useful systems will always remain obedient. No second numerical score is averaged with the extinction claim. “All imaginary” on the website is an editorial description of a dismissal being tested, not an invented quotation attributed to LeCun.
