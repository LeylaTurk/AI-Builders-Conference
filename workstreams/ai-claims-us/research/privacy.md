## Claim selection and boundaries

**Exact claim:** “ChatGPT may use personal-account conversations to train its models, depending on your settings.”

Scope: U.S. adults using ChatGPT personal accounts; policy snapshot at cutoff, with Business/Enterprise/API contrast. This is a transparent narrowing of the public privacy question, not a ranking of all chatbot services or every privacy threat.

Public occurrence: Kaycee Hill, [Tom’s Guide, updated October 30, 2025](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/keep-your-chatgpt-data-private-by-opting-out-of-training-heres-how), introduction, retrieved lines 653–656. Its opening explains that chats may improve models unless the user opts out. The selected claim preserves possibility and controls, and expressly narrows account scope. The article also makes a broader reassurance about privacy after opting out. We do **not** endorse that separate proposition or score it together with the training claim. Current provider guidance, not that older tutorial, supplies the operational advice.

Components kept distinct: (1) eligibility for model improvement; (2) storage and deletion; (3) Memory/personalization; (4) authorized company/contractor access; (5) sharing a conversation link; (6) personal versus organizational accounts. A training opt-out does not by itself answer the other questions. Serious privacy harm remains possible without making the carefully qualified central claim hyped.

## Evidence assessment

The decisive evidence is OpenAI's current published policy, including explicit training eligibility and product/settings distinctions. It is authoritative evidence of the company's stated rules, **company self-reporting rather than an independent compliance audit**. No counts of selected training conversations, probability of a privacy incident, or measurement of safeguard failures are inferred. Separate technical studies of memorization would concern another proposition; no blanket claim about verbatim disclosure of this reader's chat is made here.

Currentness matters: help pages were opened and their text inspected, not cited from search snippets. Current temporary-chat text differs from older descriptions: saving a temporary chat changes the rules and personalization can use existing context. No historical guide is used to override those current qualifications. The May 2026 corporate explainer supports a plain-language training mechanism; its older temporary-chat description is not used for current settings advice.

The policy set does not enumerate a universal untouched consumer-account default for every cohort. The page therefore directs readers to inspect their current account, while accurately identifying the affirmative no-training-by-default commitment for business services. Business use is a product/account distinction, not a synonym for entering work into a personal account. Optional controls should not be described as automatic protection.

U.S. framing: federal FTC guidance addresses deceptive commitments; CalPrivacy describes particular California rights and request processes. The explainer makes no claim of a universal U.S. deletion entitlement and avoids recently signed deletion expansions whose future effective provisions are unnecessary to this page. No individualized legal advice or guaranteed redress is implied.
