## Water/energy boundary

The water researcher inspected the [LBNL 2024 US report](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/2024-12/lbnl-2024-united-states-data-center-energy-usage-report.pdf?stream=top), printed pages 55–58, and [Virginia JLARC report](https://rga.lis.virginia.gov/Published/2025/RD206/PDF), pages 62–65. The following are researcher-reported leads for energy coordination:

- US data-center direct water consumption and modeled electricity-related consumption are separate accounting categories. Neither is an AI-only measurement.
- Withdrawal is water taken from a source; consumption is the portion not returned for immediate reuse in the same water system. Local source, season and cooling system matter.
- The LBNL 60–124-billion-liter 2028 direct-consumption range refers to hyperscale facilities, not every US data center.
- Grid-based indirect estimates do not automatically credit corporate power-purchase agreements or represent an individual resident's watershed.

Keep national denominators and local impacts separate on both pages. Let the water page explain consumption/cooling in detail and the energy page explain electricity/emissions; cross-link rather than copy entire explanations.

## Privacy currentness leads

Coordinator applied the OpenAI Docs skill for source discovery. The commission expressly requests current official service policies, so official Help Center and policy pages are appropriate even though the skill's narrower default domain list focuses on developer documentation.

Opened [Data controls in ChatGPT](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/7730893-data-controls-in-chatgpt) and [How OpenAI handles data in consumer services](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/7039943-how-openai-handles-data-in-consumer-services), October 6, 2026. Both displayed a relative update date rather than a stable absolute publication date. Search-index copies differed in title/update recency; rely on retrieved policy passages, not snippets.

Relevant passages for independent researcher inspection: data-controls sections on disabling model improvement, saved chats, temporary chats, managed workspaces, and feedback after opting out; consumer-services sections on model improvement, deletion exceptions, service providers, and authorized human access. Current temporary-chat language includes a qualification for chats subsequently saved as regular chats. Do not copy older descriptions that imply memory, training, retention and personalization are one switch. Company policies establish stated practices, not an independent compliance audit.
