# Video setup guide (first-time filming)

**AI, Seriously? · 26 September 2026 · for video 0 and the learner videos (see `09_video_plan.md`)**

Short version: an iPhone 15, good window light and a cheap clip-on microphone beat most beginner setups.

## Leyla's office: window behind and to the side

That lights the back and one side, and leaves the face in shade. Options, best first:

1. **Turn around.** Face the other way so the window is **in front of you and to one side (about 45°)**, with the phone between you and the room pointing back at you. Soft light on the face, window out of shot. The desk doesn't need to move; only the chair and tripod.
2. **Can't turn around:** soften the window with a sheer curtain or blind, and put a small **LED panel in front of you at about 45°, slightly above eye level** as the main light. The window then becomes a flattering glow on hair and shoulders. Keep the window out of the frame and lock exposure on the face.
3. **Timing:** film when the sun isn't shining straight in, or on an overcast day.

**Quick test:** hold the front camera up, turn slowly on the spot, and face the direction where your face looks brightest and most even.

## Light

- A **ring light isn't needed**. It also leaves a ring reflected in eyes and glasses.
- **Never have a window behind you** in shot: you become a silhouette.
- Turn off warm ceiling lights while filming (mixed colours look odd on skin).
- Diffuse direct sun with a sheer curtain.
- If filming in the evening (dark by about 18:30 in October) or at mixed times: a small **LED panel with a diffuser** (about $30–60) at 45°.

## Where to sit and the background

- Keep **1–2 metres between you and the back wall** for depth and no wall shadows.
- A tidy background with some depth: bookshelf, plant, lamp. A touch of the app's violet or yellow ties it to the brand.
- You don't have to film at your desk.

## Camera: iPhone 15

- **Back (main) camera** gives the best quality; the front camera is fine to start with because you can see yourself.
- **4K at 30 fps** (or 1080p to save space; 4K is about 150–200 MB a minute).
- **Tap and hold on your face** to lock focus and exposure.
- Wipe the lens; Do Not Disturb on; check free storage.
- **Tripod at eye level**, lens slightly above the eyes. Look at the lens, not the screen.

## Orientation

- **Horizontal (16:9)** for video 0: fits the app's video slot, YouTube, the judges, and the Vox style (room for graphics beside you).
- Leave space around yourself so **vertical (9:16) clips** can be cropped later for Reels, TikTok and Shorts.

## Recording and editing

- **Record in the iPhone Camera app**, not inside an editor, then import the clips.
- **Screen recordings:** Mac Cmd+Shift+5; Windows Win+G.
- **Editing:** CapCut desktop (check it's available in Türkiye); alternatives DaVinci Resolve (free) or iMovie (free on Mac).

## Sound matters most

- **USB-C clip-on (lavalier) microphone** for the iPhone 15: about $20–40.
- Soften echo with a rug, curtains, soft furniture.
- Record **10 seconds of silence** each session for noise removal.

## Starter kit (personal cost, outside the project's $100 budget)

| Item | Rough price | Needed? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phone tripod with clamp | $20–30 | Yes |
| USB-C lavalier mic | $20–40 | Strongly recommended |
| Small LED panel with diffuser | $30–60 | Only if the window can't light you from the front, or for evening filming |

## Filming tips

- Film in **short chunks**, one paragraph per take; Vox-style editing cuts often.
- Keep the script visible: bullet points beside the lens, or a teleprompter app.
- Solid colours; avoid fine stripes and pure white.
- **Do a 30-second test** and watch it on a laptop before the real session.
