How ratings work

Every rated story gets two separate scores. An AI checks each article against a published checklist, quoting the passage behind every finding. Scores are calculated from those findings, not guessed.

Story score · higher is better

How well the article is sourced, whether its key claims match their sources, and whether it keeps the important limits. Three parts, averaged: source transparency, support for claims, context.

1 Weak · 2 Limited · 3 Mixed · 4 Strong · 5 Very strong

Hype · fewer chillies is better

How far the framing goes beyond the evidence: overstated claims, hyperbole, and describing AI as if it were a person. The headline counts extra.

What a rating is not

  • Not a verdict on truth. It’s an assessment of how well this article supports what it says.
  • Low hype doesn’t mean low risk. A serious, well-evidenced warning should score strong and grounded.
  • Not about the outlet. We rate each article, because good outlets publish weak stories too.
  • Not final. Ratings are AI-assisted and can be wrong. We correct them and say so.
  • No guessing. If we can’t read the evidence, we say “Insufficient evidence to rate”.

Where the stories come from

The feed rates openly licensed journalism, university press releases and company announcements, whose terms allow it. Many big outlets don’t allow automated analysis, so we don’t fetch them. You can still check any article by pasting its text.
